Conventional Brake Components

Front Brake Pads Inspection and Replacement (cont'd)

 19-14  

  1. Remove the pad retainers.

  1. Clean the calliper thoroughly; remove any rust and check for grooves and cracks.
  2. Check the brake disc for damage and cracks.
  3. Apply Dow Corning Molykote M77 grease to the retainers on their mating surfaces against the calliper bracket.
  4. Install the pad retainers. Wipe excess grease off the retainers. Contaminated brake discs and pads reduce stopping ability. Keep grease off the discs and pads.
  5. Apply Dow Corning Molykote M77 or Daikalub 528D grease to both sides of the pad shim (A), the back of the pads (B) and the other areas indicated by the arrows.

Wipe excess grease off the shim. Contaminated brake discs and pads reduce stopping ability. Keep grease off the discs and pads.

  1. Install the brake pads and pad shim correctly. Install the pads with the wear indicators (C) on the inside.

If you are reusing the pads, always reinstall the brake pads in their original positions to prevent a momentary loss of braking efficiency.
  1. Push in the piston (A) so the calliper will fit over the pads. Make sure the piston boot is in position to prevent damaging it when pivoting the calliper down.

  1. Pivot the calliper down into position. Being careful not to damage the pin boots, install the bolt (B) and tighten it to the specified torque.
  2. Press the brake pedal several times to make sure the brake works, then test-drive.

NOTE: Engagement of the brake may require a greater pedal stroke immediately after the brake pads have been replaced as a set. Several applications of the brake pedal will restore the normal pedal stroke.

  1. After installation, check for leaks at hose and line joints or connections and retighten if necessary.
